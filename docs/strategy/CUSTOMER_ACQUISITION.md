# Customer Acquisition Plan — First 100 Paying Users

**Goal:** 100 paying subscribers within 90 days
**Audience:** Gen X pre-retirees (50-65)
**Pricing:** Free / Premium ($19/mo or $149/yr) / Planner ($29/mo or $229/yr)

---

## The Hard Truths

### 1. You're selling prevention, not pain relief

People don't wake up at 3 AM thinking "I need to optimize my Social Security claiming age." Retirement planning is important but not urgent — until it is, and by then they've already made mistakes. This means your marketing has to create urgency where none naturally exists.

### 2. The trust barrier is real and enormous

You're asking strangers on the internet to enter their income, Social Security numbers, tax details, and net worth into a website they've never heard of. For your Gen X audience (50-65), this is a massive ask. They grew up with "don't give your information to strangers online." Every financial scam targets this demographic. You are guilty until proven innocent.

### 3. The effort barrier is your silent killer

12 tools is impressive from a product standpoint and terrifying from an adoption standpoint. Even one tool takes 10-15 minutes of focused data entry. You're asking people to do homework. Most will bounce after tool #1 if they don't see immediate, personalized value.

### 4. The free tier may be too restrictive

1 scenario per tool and 2 AI questions per day — a user can't even do meaningful "what-if" analysis on the free tier. If they can't feel the power of the platform before paying, they won't pay. You need them addicted before you ask for the credit card.

### 5. $19-29/month is real money for a tool people use occasionally

Netflix is $15. Spotify is $11. Those get used daily. You need to justify a higher price for something that might get used intensively for 2-3 weeks, then monthly at best.

## The Strategy: Three Concurrent Channels

Don't try to boil the ocean. You need exactly 100 paying users. Here's how, in order of expected ROI.

---

## Channel 1: The Advisor Flywheel

**Target: 40-50 paying users**

This is the highest-leverage channel. One advisor can bring 10-20 clients.

### Why it works

You've already built the advisor tier. Advisors have a pre-existing trust relationship with their clients. When an advisor says "use this tool," the trust barrier evaporates. The data entry feels like homework your advisor assigned, not a random internet form.

### The play

- Find 5-10 independent financial advisors or fee-only planners who serve pre-retirees
- Offer them free Advisor tier for 12 months — no strings
- Their clients get Premium tier free for 90 days (then convert to paid)
- The advisor gets a dashboard showing their clients' readiness scores — this is genuinely useful to them, not a gimmick

### Where to find them

- NAPFA (National Association of Personal Financial Advisors) directory — fee-only planners who don't sell products
- XY Planning Network — serves younger/tech-forward advisors
- Local CFP meetups or LinkedIn outreach
- r/financialplanning — advisors lurk here

### The pitch to advisors

> "Your clients are going to plan their retirement with or without a tool. Right now they're using spreadsheets or nothing. RetireMore gives you visibility into their full picture — income, taxes, healthcare, Social Security — in one dashboard. You can see their readiness score and where the gaps are. It makes your next meeting 10x more productive."

### Expected conversion

5 advisors x 10 clients each x 60% convert after trial = **30 paying users**

## Channel 2: Content-Led SEO + Community

**Target: 30-40 paying users**

### Why it works

Your target audience Googles specific retirement questions. They're not searching "retirement planning tool" — they're searching "should I claim Social Security at 62 or 67" and "how much will Medicare cost me." These are high-intent, low-competition long-tail queries.

### The play

- Write 10-15 articles that answer specific retirement questions with real numbers
- Each article naturally leads to one of your tools as the "calculate your specific number" CTA
- Publish on a /blog subdomain or Medium, cross-post to relevant subreddits

### Article ideas that map to tools

| Article | Tool CTA |
|---------|----------|
| "The $100K Social Security mistake most people make" | SS Optimizer |
| "What Medicare actually costs in 2026 (with IRMAA brackets)" | Healthcare Calculator |
| "States that don't tax retirement income — the complete list" | State Relocator |
| "How to know if you can actually afford to retire" | Income Planner |
| "The Roth conversion window most pre-retirees miss" | Tax Analyzer |

### Where to post

- r/retirement (32K members, your exact audience)
- r/financialplanning (200K+ members)
- r/Fire (for early retirement crowd — younger but engaged)
- Bogleheads forum (extremely high-quality retirement discussion)
- Facebook groups: "Retirement Planning" groups have 50K-200K members

### Critical rule

Lead with genuine value, not promotion. Answer the question fully in the post. The tool is the "want your specific number?" follow-up, not the pitch.

### Expected timeline

2-3 months to see organic traffic. This is a slow burn but compounds.

### Expected conversion

500 organic visitors/month x 10% signup x 15% convert to paid = ~8 paying users/month after month 3

## Channel 3: Direct Outreach + Partnerships

**Target: 20-30 paying users**

At the 0-100 stage, things that don't scale are your best friend.

### a) HR departments at companies with retirement-age employees

- Target companies with 200-2000 employees (big enough to have HR, small enough to care)
- Position RetireMore as a pre-retirement benefit
- Offer bulk pricing: company pays $12/user/month (discount), employees get Premium
- 1 HR partnership = 20-50 users overnight

### b) Retirement workshops and webinars

- Partner with local libraries, community centers, or credit unions to run free "Retirement Readiness" workshops
- Live-demo the Income Planner with real (anonymized) scenarios
- Attendees get a 30-day free Premium trial
- This builds trust through face-to-face interaction — devastating for your demographic

### c) Credit union and community bank partnerships

- Credit unions serve your exact demographic and are always looking for member benefits
- Offer white-label or co-branded access
- They promote it to their members, you handle the tech

## Tactical Fixes to Improve Conversion

These are changes to the product/pricing that will directly impact your ability to convert.

### 1. Loosen the free tier

Give away 3 scenarios and 5 AI questions/day. Right now free users can't do enough to get hooked. Let them run 3 scenarios on the Income Planner. Let them ask the AI Coach enough questions to feel how powerful it is. The "aha moment" needs to happen before the paywall, not after.

### 2. Build a "Retirement Readiness Report" that's free and shareable

After completing 2-3 tools, generate a beautiful one-page summary with their readiness score, top 3 risks, and top 3 opportunities. Make it downloadable and shareable. This becomes:
- The hook ("Get your free retirement readiness score")
- Social proof when people share it
- The conversion trigger ("Unlock your full plan — upgrade to Premium")

### 3. Reduce first-tool friction dramatically

The Income Planner should have a "Quick Start" mode: 5 fields, 2 minutes, approximate results. Then say "Want more accurate projections? Add your full details." Progressive disclosure beats a 15-minute form every time.

### 4. Add social proof immediately

You have zero reviews, zero testimonials, zero trust signals. Before spending a dollar on marketing:
- Get 5 beta users to write honest testimonials
- Add "Used by X pre-retirees" counter (even if X is small, honesty builds trust)
- Add security badges, SOC2 mention (if applicable), "Your data is encrypted" messaging
- Add "We're Eddie & Lara" — put your faces on the landing page. Gen X trusts people, not brands

### 5. Offer annual pricing at launch with a founding member discount

"First 100 members: $99/year for Premium (normally $149)." Creates urgency, locks in revenue, and the word "founding" makes early adopters feel special.

## The One Thing That Will Kill You

**Not talking to users.** At 0-100, you should be personally emailing every single user who signs up. Ask them what confused them. Ask them what almost made them leave. Ask them what they'd pay for. The Loops drip sequences are great for scale, but right now you need to be in their inbox personally.

Your first 100 users are not customers. They're co-builders. Treat them that way.
