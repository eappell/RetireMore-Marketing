# Audience Personas

Five working personas. Use these in copy, ad targeting, sequence segmentation, and outreach lists. Each persona maps to a primary entry point (the tool most likely to bring them in) and a secondary expansion path.

## 1. The Pre-Retiree Researcher — "Linda, 58"

**Profile:** Senior individual contributor or middle manager. Earns $90K–$180K. Has been saving in a 401(k) for 25+ years. Sees retirement coming in 5–8 years and is starting to take it seriously.

**Goals:** Know if she's on track. Understand Social Security claiming. Avoid expensive mistakes.
**Fears:** Outliving her money. Healthcare costs. Making an irreversible decision (claiming SS too early, the wrong Medicare plan).
**Watering holes:** Reddit r/retirement, r/financialindependence, NYT Your Money, Kitces blog, Morningstar, NerdWallet, The Retirement Manifesto, podcasts (Stacking Benjamins, Retire Sooner).
**Triggers:** Annual benefits statement arrives, hits a milestone birthday (55, 60), a colleague retires.
**Primary tool:** Retirement Income Planner
**Secondary tools:** Social Security Optimization, Healthcare Cost Calculator, Tax Impact Analyzer
**Voice fit:** Specific numbers, honest about trade-offs, no salesy hype.

## 2. The Newly Retired Optimizer — "Mark, 64"

**Profile:** Just retired or about to. Has a portfolio between $500K–$2M. Probably worked with a fee-only advisor at some point but wants to verify the math himself. May still have a working spouse.

**Goals:** Optimize Roth conversions in the gap years before RMDs. Sequence withdrawals tax-efficiently. Avoid IRMAA cliffs. Decide where to live.
**Fears:** Sequence-of-returns risk in early retirement. Tax surprises. Medicare IRMAA brackets.
**Watering holes:** Bogleheads forum, Kitces, Boldin (formerly NewRetirement), Pralana, ProjectionLab, Rob Berger YouTube, Big ERN's Safe Withdrawal Rate Series.
**Triggers:** Retirement date, year-end Roth conversion planning, an unexpected RMD calculation.
**Primary tool:** Tax Impact Analyzer
**Secondary tools:** Longevity & Drawdown Planner, Retirement Income Planner, State Relocate Selector
**Voice fit:** Treat him as smart. Specific, technical, source-cited. No condescension.

## 3. The Expat-Curious — "Janet & David, 62 & 65"

**Profile:** Couple. Empty nesters. Have traveled enough to know they want to live abroad part- or full-time. Researching Portugal, Mexico, Costa Rica, Spain, Thailand, Panama. Care about cost, safety, healthcare, visa, community.

**Goals:** Pick a country (or 2–3 to visit). Understand the tax implications. Figure out healthcare. Decide whether to keep a US base.
**Fears:** Healthcare quality outside the US. Visa renewals. Tax double-dipping. Being far from grandchildren.
**Watering holes:** International Living, Expat Exchange, r/expats, r/IWantOut, Nomad List, country-specific Facebook groups, YouTube expat vloggers (Hayes & Tanner, Our Freedom Years, Retired & Travelin').
**Triggers:** A trip abroad, a friend who moved, a US tax/insurance/property frustration.
**Primary tool:** Retire Abroad AI
**Secondary tools:** Healthcare Cost Calculator (for the gap years before Medicare abroad), Tax Impact Analyzer
**Voice fit:** Adventure-positive but practical. Honest about visa/healthcare gotchas. No "best country to retire in" listicle clichés.

## 4. The Identity-Searcher — "Robert, 67"

**Profile:** Retired or near retirement from a long career (engineer, lawyer, executive, professor, military). Financially fine. The money question doesn't keep him up at night — the *who am I now* question does.

**Goals:** Find purpose. Build a new daily structure. Stay engaged. Maybe start something — consulting, teaching, board work, volunteering, a small business.
**Fears:** Becoming irrelevant. Decline. Boredom. Marriage strain from being home all day.
**Watering holes:** Modern Elder Academy, NextAvenue, Atul Gawande / Arthur Brooks reading, AARP, Encore.org, New York Times Well section, podcasts (How to Live, Retire Sooner with purpose episodes).
**Triggers:** Retirement date, a friend's death, a "what do I do today" moment in the first few months.
**Primary tool:** Retirement Identity Builder
**Secondary tools:** Volunteer Purpose Matchmaker, Legacy Flow Visualizer
**Voice fit:** Reflective, respectful, not glib. The hard work is psychological, not arithmetic.

## 5. The Estate Organizer — "Patricia, 71"

**Profile:** Widowed or thinking about it. Has assets to leave behind — house, IRA, brokerage, possibly a small business. Wants to make it easy for her kids and avoid family fights.

**Goals:** Get the estate plan in order. Document everything. Make sure heirs know what's where. Maybe start gifting now (annual exclusion, education).
**Fears:** Probate. Family conflict. The kids inheriting a mess. Cognitive decline making decisions impossible later.
**Watering holes:** AARP estate planning, Trust & Will, Wealthfront/Schwab estate articles, attorney blogs, NextAvenue legacy section.
**Triggers:** A spouse's diagnosis, a friend's estate going badly, a milestone birthday (70, 75).
**Primary tool:** Digital Estate Manager
**Secondary tools:** Legacy Flow Visualizer, Gifting Strategy Planner
**Voice fit:** Calm, organized, warm. This is emotional work. Lead with peace of mind, not tax efficiency.
